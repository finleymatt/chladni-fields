## Description (4000)

A vibrating plate scattered with sand sorts itself into figures, the sand settling wherever the plate holds still. Chladni Fields runs that idea backwards.

Famous crop formations, and the ancient figures they echo, are read as sound. The geometry of each one sets a fundamental and a stack of partials, and beside it is the pattern that frequency would raise in sand on Ernst Chladni's plate. Play the tone. Hear what Milk Hill, the Julia Set, the Barbury Castle π formation or the Flower of Life sound like.

Then raise your own. The free plate listens: set a tone with the slider, or sing, hum, whistle or play an instrument into the microphone, and the plate answers in real time. Low notes make big lazy loops; high notes make fine filigree. This is a real plate model, not a filter: every vibration mode responds by how close it sits to resonance with what it hears.

WHAT'S INSIDE
• Fourteen formations and six ancient figures, each with its fundamental, partials, symmetry, span, and a dossier with the original image, location and sources
• Two plates side by side: the round plate scaled to the figure's span, and the free square plate for your own tone
• Real Chladni physics: forced-vibration modal superposition, Bessel modes on the round plate, and sand that settles along the nodal lines
• A pitch detector and spectrum analyser that follow your voice or instrument live
• "Why this shape" explains, for every figure, how close the plate can come to the original and why
• The Resonance Room: 174, 432, 528 and 963 Hz and more, binaural beats, brown and pink noise, three timbres, a breathing guide and a fade-out timer, with the research behind each tone labelled honestly and footnoted
• The Law of One in nine readings, with the sessions they come from
• Three themes: dark, light, and a visionary palette

Nothing leaves your device. The microphone is analysed live and discarded; there are no accounts, no analytics, and no ads. Works offline.
