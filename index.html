Eight of the best-known crop formations are read as sound: the geometry sets a fundamental and a stack of partials, and beside each one is the pattern that frequency would raise in sand on Ernst Chladni's plate.

How the translation works

Five readings off one drawing

  1. Span → pitch.The formation's overall diameter sets the fundamental. A wider mark in the crop sounds lower, the way a longer string or a larger plate does.
  2. Symmetry → harmony.N-fold rotational symmetry lifts the Nth partial and its multiples — the same partials a plate divides into when it carries N nodal diameters.
  3. Rings → timbre.Concentric bands in the design push energy higher or lower in the partial stack, giving each formation its colour.
  4. Lay → attack.A swept, spiral lay rolls the upper partials off gently and warm; a hard geometric lay keeps them bright and present.
  5. Then the plate.The fundamental is fed to a square-plate Chladni model. The nodal lines it draws are the still places — where the sand would gather.
